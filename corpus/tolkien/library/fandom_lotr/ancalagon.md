# Ancalagon

Source: https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Ancalagon

" It has been said that dragon-fire could melt and consume the Rings of Power, but there is not now any dragon left on earth in which the old fire is hot enough; nor was there ever any dragon, not even Ancalagon the Black, who could have harmed the One Ring, the Ruling Ring, for that was made by Sauron himself. " — Gandalf to Frodo Baggins in The Lord of the Rings , The Fellowship of the Ring , Book One, ch. II: " The Shadow of the Past " Ancalagon , also known as Anddraca , the Great Dragon of Morgoth , the Black Dragon , or the Black , was the "greatest of the winged dragons of Morgoth " and "mightiest of the dragon-host" during the War of Wrath . He was bred by Morgoth during the First Age and possibly was the largest winged dragon to have ever existed in Middle-earth . [ citation needed ] Biography During the late First Age , the Wise-woman Andreth , of the Edain , once made a prophecy "that Túrin " would return before leaving the Circles of the World and slay Ancalagon during the Last Battle . It was said that the extinct "language of the Folk of Haleth" would only be heard again if Andreth's prophecy came true, but whether her prophecy referred to the Last Battle at the end of the First Age , or the Last Battle at the Ambar-metta , is untold. After Eärendil made his journey to Valinor to convince the Valar to overthrow Morgoth and rescue the overmatched and defeated Elves and Edain , the Host of Valinor made war upon Morgoth and destroyed his armies during the War of Wrath . In the year 587 , Morgoth unleashed his last and greatest weapon; a fleet of winged dragons, which he had been hiding in Angband. They were "winged with steel " and Ancalagon was among them. Their onslaught was so terrible that the armies of the Valar were driven back to Dor-nu-Fauglith from the gates of Angband, and their coming was said to have been accompanied by great thunderbolts, lightning, and fire storms. But Eärendil came out of the West in his blessed ship Vingilot , accompanied by "a myriad" of "all the great birds of heaven" led by their captain, Thorondor , an Eagle . The Eagles and Eärendil dueled with Ancalagon and the other dragons for a day and a night. Before the next sunrise, Eärendil was victorious "and overthrew Ancalagon", casting him down "upon the towers of Thangorodrim ", destroying them in his fall. The death of Ancalagon marked the end of Morgoth's final resistance. Legacy On April 13 in the year 3018 of the late Third Age , after Frodo Baggins chastised Gandalf in Bag End , Hobbiton for not sending a message to The Shire to tell him that the One Ring must be destroyed, Gandalf dared Frodo to try and destroy it, only for Frodo to discover that he did not want to harm it. Gandalf explained that even if Frodo had the will to destroy it, there was no way to do so. Neither the forges of the Dwarves nor any type of Dragon, including Ancalagon, could harm it. Etymology Ancalagon , or Anc-alagon , is a Sindarin "dragon-name" meaning "rushing jaws" or "Biting-Storm". It contains the words anc ("bite; jaws; jaw, row of teeth") , alagon / alagos ("storm of wind") , and the root alak- ("rushing") . In Eriol 's Old English translations, Ancalagon is referred as Anddraca ("Enemy-dragon"), derived from the elements and- ("opposition, negation"), anda ("enmity, hatred, envy"), and draca ("dragon"). Draca is an Old English word derived from Latin. In other versions The first appearance of Ancalagon in J.R.R. Tolkien 's legendarium was in the Quenta Noldorinwa from the 1930s, which gives a version even more summarized of the War of Wrath than what is given above, although the story does not differ from later versions. Tolkien never fully completed the Quenta Silmarillion , so the information about Ancalagon was never developed, and although he revised the text of the War of Wrath, they were very brief expansions, made in the Quenta Silmarillion of 1937 , and in "The Later Quenta Silmarillion " of 1951 . This last version was the main text used by Christopher Tolkien for his edition of The Silmarillion . The only mention of Ancalagon in later texts is in a note about the language of the Haladin, written in Tolkien's last years. In the untitled first version of the chapter " The Shadow of the Past " in The Lord of the Rings , Ancalagon isn't mentioned though Gandalf still explains to Bingo that there is no type of Dragon that could harm the One Ring. Inspiration On September 30 of 2000 , Jonathan Evans suggested in an essay that the battle between Eärendil and Ancalagon may have been inspired by the battle between Satan and the Archangel Michael in the Book of Revelation . In 2007 , John D. Rateliff stated that Ancalagon's role during the War of Wrath may be an analogue to Miðgarðsormr 's ("Dragon of the Island-earth" or "Middle-earth Wyrm") role during Ragnarök in the Prose Edda . On May 5 of 2009 , Christopher Tolkien explained that Túrin slaying Ancalagon in Andreth's prophecy resembled Sigurd killing Miðgarðsormr in the Völuspá , especially in the retelling his father made in the Völsungakviða en nýja eða Sigurðarkviða en mesta . According to Kristine Larsen , the confrontation is a euhemeric rendering of a meteor shower falling on the planet Venus, the latter of which is described as the star corresponding to Eärendil in Tolkien's mythology. Speculation and analysis On October 4 of 2010 , a user of The Barrow-Downs Discussion Forum , Vultur, speculated that Ancalagon was probably "a mile long at least". On October 6 , a user called narfforc pointed out that Ancalagon's might doesn’t necessarily mean his size. Ancalagon's size is not specified, but must have been colossal due to the destruction of "the towers of Thangorodrim" when he fell on them, which are elsewhere identified with the three smoking peaks of those mountains. However, his size cannot be assumed to be larger than Thangorodrim; [ citation needed ] some powerful but comparatively small creatures are able to cause great destruction even in death, such as Durin's Bane breaking the slopes of Celebdil . His size would however, still have needed to have been great enough to strike and destroy all three peaks as he fell, [ citation needed ] and each mountain was about five miles across. [ citation needed ] Impact In 1977 , an extinct genus of worms found in the The Walcott Quarry on Fossil Ridge in 1911 dating from the Middle Cambrian Era from the Cambrian Burgess Shale was named Ancalagon , inspired by Tolkien's dragon. Two other animals are named after Ancalagon: Ancalagon , a Cambrian priapulid by Simon Conway Morris in 1977 , and Ankalagon by Leigh Van Valen in 1980 , a Paleocene mesonychid mammal. In 2000 , a French pagan metal band used Ancalagon as their name. In adaptations Middle-earth Role Playing In the Middle-earth Role Playing game, Ancalagon the Black was a winged Fire-drake who was the first of his line and the most powerful Dragon to have ever existed. He was also known as Rushing-jaws and the Black Dragon. Ancalagon "was far larger" than Glaurung , possessed wings that "darkened the skies", and caused "hurricane-winds" to sweep across plains before unleashing "unstoppable fire". Being "hundreds of feet long" and "capable of destroying an entire forest in a single blast" of fire, Ancalagon could "slay whole tribes unaided" or cause an army to flee. The spells he could cast "rivalled those of the Maiar and his" appearance was as terrifying as one of the Balrogs . Thus, he was considered to be "the epitome of Fire-drakes and the father of Winged Worms"; "the culmination of Dragon-breeding" and the greatest monster to walk Middle-earth . Gothmog was the only one of Morgoth's servant who could match him. During the War of Wrath at the end of the First Age, Ancalagon was unleashed to defend Thangorodrim from foes. In this Great Battle, "he dueled with the Great Eagles" alongside his fellow Dragons. As a consequence of the duel, smoke turned the skies black and many clouds were broken apart by thunder. While this "unparalleled aerial fray" commenced, Eärendil cut Ancalagon "down with a remarkable bowshot". This "lucky and skillful" shot created "an upheaval" among his fellow Dragons as they "fled amidst the turmoil" when his fall "shattered the peaks below". His huge size broke "the roof of" Thangorodrim "like a skyborne earthquake" and crushed its "spine" beyond repair. Though Ancalagon died, he had three sons who survived the War of Wrath: Ruingurth, Smaug the Golden , and Throkmaw the Black.
