# Bladorthin

Source: https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Bladorthin

" ...the spears that were made for the armies of the great King Bladorthin (long since dead), each had a thrice-forged head and their shafts were inlaid with cunning gold , but they were never delivered or paid for... " — The Hobbit , " Inside Information " Bladorthin was a great king of an unknown location within Middle-earth , sometime in the Third Age . Biography Sometime during the mid- to late Third Age, Bladorthin ruled a kingdom which maintained armies and had an unspecified trade relationship with the Dwarven Kingdom under the Mountain . For an unspecified reason, Bladorthin charged the Dwarves of Erebor with making multiple spears for his armies. These spears were to each consist of "a thrice-forged head" with a shaft cunningly lined with gold . While the spears were indeed forged, the Dwarves never delivered them, and Bladorthin never paid. As a consequence, the spears remained within Erebor. In the year 2941 during the Quest of Erebor long after Bladorthin had passed away, Thorin and Balin pondered whether or not Bladorthin's order was still intact, possibly implying that Bladorthin's realm was close in proximity to the Lonely Mountain . [ citation needed ] Etymology It is possible that Bladorthin is a Sindarin name comprised of the elements blador ("world") and thin(n) ("pale; grey") a variation of thind . While J.R.R. Tolkien never elaborated on its meaning, John D. Rateliff noted that the name was "clearly Gnomish (or perhaps Noldorin )". If this was the case, then Bladorthin would translate to "Grey Country", "Grey Plains Fay", or "Grey Master of the Plains" and therefore may be comprised of the Gnomish elements blador ("wide, flat, open country") and -thin ("grey"). Rateliff also suggested that, if blador were interpreted less literally as "far and wide", then Bladorthin may mean "Grey Wanderer". Like Rateliff, Andreas Möhn has also identified the name as Gnomish. However, he claimed that blador meant "wide earth, flat earth" and interpreted the whole name as "wide earth-grey" or "Grey One of the wide lands". He also speculated that the Quenya form of Bladorthin may have been Palursindo . Unlike them, Michael L. Martinez did not believe that Bladorthin was Gnomish, noting not only that the element -blad only appeared in Bladorion , a name which Tolkien abandoned, but also that the element is similar in sound to "Blaed(a)", an Old English name for "renowned". Mark Fisher speculated on The Encyclopedia of Arda that Bladorthin was an Elvish name meaning "wide grey plain" from blador ("wide plain") and thin ("grey"). In other versions In the early drafts of The Hobbit , Bladorthin was the name of Gandalf (who would later be assigned the colour "grey" in The Lord of the Rings ) for much of the narrative until Tolkien decided to change it late in the writing process. A connection can still be seen with the element -thin ("grey"). In The History of The Hobbit: Mr Baggins and Return to Bag-End , John D. Rateliff contrasts Bladorthin with Gandalf as "a traditional fairy-tale enchanter" who rescued "many princesses, earls, dukes, widow's sons and fair maidens", slayed "unlamented giants", and "turned the dragon of the Far Mountains inside out". In adaptations The Hobbit: The Prelude to The Lord of the Rings In the 2003 video game, The Hobbit: The Prelude to The Lord of the Rings , Bladorthin's spears are among a list of items that Bilbo Baggins must locate within Erebor during a quest in the "Inside Information" chapter. Speculation It has been theorized by Robert Foster that Bladorthin was an Elven king who had a premature death, therefore preventing the trade. Thus narrowing his death to between the years 1999 and 2770 . This theory is shared by J.E.A. Tyler [ citation needed ] and Michael L. Martinez , though Martinez also adds that he may have been a King of Dale who reigned long before Girion . This theory that Bladorthin was a mortal Man is shared by Douglas A. Anderson and Mark Fisher , who added that Bladorthin was probably born before the year 2770 , and that his realm was probably not close to Erebor. The Tolkienwiki not only agrees with the theory of Bladorthin having a premature death, but also theorize that his kingdom was located around the Sea of Rhûn before being destroyed by Sauron . Andreas Möhn disagrees with most of the above theories, particularly the belief of his premature death since The Hobbit never mentioned Bladorthin's death as being premature. As a result, Möhn theorizes that Smaug's arrival in the year 2770 prevented the trade, implying that Bladorthin's death must have been sometime later. He also countered the theory that he was a King of Dale because Dale was never a kingdom before Bard . Möhn therefore identifies that his kingdom was actually located in Dorwinion . In addition, he also suggests that Bladorthin may have been related to the Kings Thorin mentioned who used to send for the smiths of Erebor "and reward even the least skillful" generously. Möhn later incorporated his theory into his Middle-earth Science Pages .
