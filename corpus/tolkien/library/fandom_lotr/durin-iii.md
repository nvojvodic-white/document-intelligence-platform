# Durin III

Source: https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Durin_III

Durin III was a King of Durin's Folk , and the first bearer of the mightiest of the seven Dwarf Rings , though this was not known to outsiders until the end of the Third Age . Biography As a descendant of Durin I who bore the same name, Durin III was considered by many Dwarves to be the reincarnation of the original Durin due to the great similarity in appearance and manner. Sometime shortly after SA 1500 , Durin received one of the Seven rings ; the Dwarves of Durin's Folk claimed the ring was given by Celebrimbor himself, and not by Sauron , though Sauron was involved in the making. This was the ring that Thráin II later carried and had taken from him when he was captured by the agents of Sauron. During the War of the Elves and Sauron the Dark Lord 's forces were on the point of overtaking and destroying the refugees and army of Elrond , when the Dwarves of Khazad-dûm sortied from the Doors of Durin and attacked Sauron's forces in the rear, giving the Elves time to escape and earning the Dark Lord's enmity. After this time the gates of Khazad-dûm were shut. In adaptations The Lord of the Rings film trilogy Durin III appears momentarily in the prologue of The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring as he receives one of the Seven Dwarf-rings. Video games In The Lord of the Rings Online , Durin III appears briefly in a flashback, ordering the West-gate of Moria sealed after the Sack of Eregion . The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power Durin appears in a much greater capacity in Amazon Studios ' The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power during his days ruling Khazad-dûm than is described in the books. Season One In Season 1 , he here is portrayed as the father of Durin IV ; a more traditionally-minded Dwarf in contrast to his son, who is willing to seek alliances with other peoples. After Durin IV hosts his friend Elrond for dinner and hears of the Elf's proposal, he brings the news to his father, who is skeptical and expresses a desire to remain isolated. He relents after much persuasion by Elrond and allows his people to begin building the great forge requested by Celebrimbor in Ost-in-Edhil . Concurrently, Durin IV has discovered veins of a new ore in an old mine underneath Kheled-zâram to which Elrond gives the name mithril . After a shaft collapse which nearly kills several dwarves, though, Durin III shuts down the operation, angering the younger Durin. After Durin IV learns of the Elves' plight, and of their perceived need for mithril, he convinces his father to listen to Elrond's proposal of a trade for the precious metal. The king refuses, however, believing the endeavor would endanger his people. He is angrily confronted by his son, who pushes too far in his pleas and causes Durin III to strip him of his title as prince. When Durin IV is discovered secretly helping Elrond mine for mithril, his father throws Elrond out of the kingdom at the West-gate , despite the two discovering an enormous vein of the metal. Season Two At the beginning of Season 2 , Durin III is a strong but increasingly stubborn ruler. His kingdom thrives on the mining of mithril , the rare and powerful metal found deep beneath the mountains. When the Dwarves receive one of the Seven Rings, forged through the schemes of Sauron (while disguised as Annatar), Durin III becomes one of the first Dwarf-lords to wear a Ring of Power . Unlike the Rings given to Men, the Dwarven rings do not fully dominate their wearers, but they amplify traits already present — especially pride, possessiveness, and a hunger for treasure. Under the Ring’s influence, Durin III grows more obsessed with expanding Khazad-dûm’s wealth and power. He begins to distrust outside allies and grows increasingly authoritarian within his own court. His relationship with his son, Durin IV, becomes strained as the prince questions the risks of digging too deeply and the growing greed surrounding the mithril mines. Durin III believes that the Ring is a gift meant to strengthen the Dwarves and secure their future. However, his fixation on wealth and power blinds him to the subtle corruption spreading through the kingdom. While he still sees himself as a protector of his people, his decisions push Khazad-dûm closer to danger — both politically and deep beneath the mountains. Finally, after many of his dwarves, including Narvi , turned against him, the king decides to go down himself to dig. When the Balrog emerges, Durin III finally realizes the Ring has been clouding his judgment. In his last act as king, he removes the Ring of Power and hands authority to his son. Looking back at his son, he confronts the Balrog alone. Armed with his axe and standing before the creature in the depths of the mountain, Durin III charges into battle. The clash triggers a violent collapse in the mines, and the king is killed while fighting the Balrog, sacrificing himself to save his son and rebury the Balrog. Some viewer's may think this Balrog was named Durin's Bane due to the death of Durin III, but canonically the namesake was Durin VI who lived much later in the Third Age.
