# Long-worms

Long-worms were either a type of dragon in northern Middle-earth or another name that J.R.R. Tolkien used for, at least, some of them. History Long-worms are inferred to originate from the pits of Angband like the other dragons of Arda . The only long-worm mentioned by name was Scatha , the great dragon of Ered Mithrin . He was slain by Fram of the Éothéod in approximately the year 2000 of the Third Age . For a long time afterwards, the long-worms did not pose any threat to the land. It is unknown whether Scatha was the last of the long-worms or if some still existed. The word 'Worm' was used to describe Glaurung , , Scatha and Smaug , but it is uncertain whether this refers to the long-worms as well. Speculation Tolkien's dragons tend to have more of a sinuous serpentine form then that of a traditional Western dragon and more like a winged snake. This could explain the name long-worm. It is possible that long-worms were wingless dragons that lived in the Northern Waste during the Third Age. They may have terrorized the Men and Dwarves that lived in the area for many hundreds of years capturing great hoards of treasure. They may have ranged from silver to rich gold to deep red or even black and breathe mist and flame. It might be possible that Were-worms in the folk lore of Hobbits may have been based on long-worms. The following was once suggested on Tolkien Gateway years ago, but without citations: It has been suggested that the Long-worms were original creatures created by Ilúvatar, and that the dragons of Morgoth were bred from them, as something cannot be bred from nothing. This suggestion also implies that the long-worms were witless hunters, like lions or wolves, with the attraction for bright things, like ravens, rather than intelligent beings like the later dragons.
