Sea-serpents or fish-dragons were a breed of fish-like Dragons mentioned in The Lost Road and Other Writings . History Sea-serpents lived in fresh water or in the Sea . Not appearing in any tale set in Arda , they are only mentioned in The Lost Road and Other Writings , in the section concerning Elvish etymologies. Etymology Lingwilókë is a Quenya word meaning "fish-dragon, sea-serpent", being comprised of lingwe ("fish") and lókë ("dragon"). The Ñoldorin cognate of lingwilókë is lhimlug , being comprised of lhim ("fish") and Lhûg ("dragon"). In other versions In Roverandom , a Sea-serpent lived in a cave known as Pot at the bottom of the Deep Blue Sea . It is an extremely enormous and powerful creature, more than one hundred miles long, allegedly causing storms just by passing through the world. The Man-in-the-Moon had once tried to seal the Sea-serpent with his magic, but took fifty years to create the proper magic, and succeeded, though accidentally causing an entire continent to sink. During the time of the story, the wizard, Mr. Artaxerxes, received written Complaints about the Sea-serpent allegedly waking up and moving to a new den that is very close to the mer-king's palace. Unfortunately, when Mr. Artaxerxes went to deal with it, Roverandom followed him and bit the tail of a shark in the shell carriage, causing a domino effect which ended with the last shark in the carriage biting the Sea-serpent. The awakening was further accelerated when Mr. Artaxerxes tried to put it back to sleep, causing it to dream that it was being burnt alive by a volcano. Earthquakes and hurricanes followed as the Sea-serpent continuously rolled over until he spoke loudly that he would only go back to sleep if Mr. Artaxerxes was banished. The Mer-people accepted his terms and banished the wizard. In adaptations The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power In The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power , a creature known only as "The Worm"—destroys a ship from the Southlands of Middle-earth , leaving seven survivors stranded on a makeshift raft in the Sundering Seas . After two weeks adrift, the castaways — Halbrand , Eamon , Abigail , Astrid , and two others—encounter a stranded Elf , Galadriel , and bring her aboard. At that very moment, Astrid spots what appears to be an approaching ship, but Abigail soon realizes it is the wreckage of their own vessel, dragged through the water by the worm. In the ensuing attack, the creature kills or scatters all but Galadriel and Halbrand, leaving them the sole survivors of the encounter. The worm later reemerges during the Trial by Abyss , choosing to spare Míriel. Middle-earth Role Playing In Middle-earth Role Playing , Sea-serpents, known as Water Drakes or Water Serpents , appear in two different forms. The first form, known as Rain-drakes or Lake-worms , are a smaller variety that live only in fresh water. The second form, known as True Water-drakes or True Sea Serpents , are a larger variety that live in both fresh water and in the sea. Furthermore, one particular True Water-drake called Séahmatha ("Lake Serpent" in a Northern language) dwells within the Long Lake . Middle-earth Collectible Card Game In the Middle-earth Collectible Card Game , Sea-serpents are considered to be of Drake-kind , being referred to as Sea Serpents and Rain-drakes . One particular Sea Serpent that inhabits coastal regions and sites is named Eärcaraxë .
