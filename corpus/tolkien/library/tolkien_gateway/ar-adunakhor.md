# Ar-Adûnakhôr

Source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Ar-Ad%C3%BBnakh%C3%B4r

Ar-Adûnakhôr was the son of King Tar-Ardamin and the twentieth King of Númenor.


== History ==
Adûnakhôr was born in a time when most Númenóreans already had stopped using the Elven tongues generations before, although it was still customary for the Kings of Númenor to take their royal names in Quenya.
When Ar-Adûnakhôr succeeded his father, however, he was the first King to break with tradition and took his title in Adûnaic. Tar-Herunúmen, a name in Quenya was still recorded in the Scroll of Kings, because the kings feared that a break with this ancient custom could cause evil to befall.
His names were perceived as blasphemous and ill-omened by the Faithful Númenóreans, because they mean "Lord of the West", a title that the Faithful only used for one of the Valar, especially Manwë.
During his reign the Elven-tongues were no longer used, nor permitted to be taught, but were maintained in secret by the Faithful. After that elven ships from Tol Eressëa only came seldom and secretly to the west shores of Númenor.
Númenórean settlers in the north of Middle-earth (between the Gulf of Lune and the city of Pelargir) refused to participate in the rebellion against the Valar and were joined by many exiles of the Faithful who fled from persecution by Ar-Adûnakhôr and the later Kings of Númenor. During the reign of Ar-Adûnakhôr the term "Middle Men" originated from these settlers.
He was succeeded to the throne by his son, Ar-Zimrathôn.


== Etymology and names ==
Adûnakhôr is an Adûnaic name that means "Lord of the West". It can be analyzed as adûn "West" (S. dûn) and khôr "Lord" (Q. heru). It is said that as a title, it signifies Manwë. Although the name was taken in defiance of Quenya, ironically its elements are said to be of Elvish origin. See also ar-.
Herunúmen is a Quenya name that means "Lord of the West". Ostensibly a compound of heru ("lord") and númen ("west"). Like for all the rulers of Númenor whose name was inscribed in the Scroll of Kings, the prefix tar- ("high") was added to his name.
