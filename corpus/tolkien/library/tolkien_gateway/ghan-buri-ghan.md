# Ghân-buri-Ghân

Source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gh%C3%A2n-buri-Gh%C3%A2n

Ghân-buri-Ghân was the great headman of the Drúedain who inhabited Drúadan Forest in the late Third Age.


== History ==
Ghân-buri-Ghân was the son of Ghân with whom he shared his name, and became the great headman of his people.

On 13 March T.A. 3019 during the War of the Ring, Ghân-buri-Ghân met with King Théoden and the Rohirrim. The great headman of the Woses revealed the existence of the Gondrant, an old route running through the Stonewain Valley from the quarries beneath Min-Rimmon to Minas Anor. During the next day, Ghân-buri-Ghân guided the Rohirrim along the road to avoid the Orcs blocking the main road so they could reach Minas Tirith. This allowed the Rohirrim to arrive in time to fight the Battle of the Pelennor Fields. In return, Théoden promised that the people of Rohan would no longer trouble the Drúedain and would drive away the Gorgûn that hunted them.
Following the War of the Ring, Aragorn, as High King of the Reunited Kingdom, issued the decree that the Drúadan Forest belonged to only Ghân-buri-Ghân and his people, and that no one else was allowed to enter the forest without their permission.


== Etymology ==
Ghân-buri-Ghân means "Ghân son of Ghân" in the language of the Drúedain. It is a compound of the name Ghân, the element buri ("son of") and the name Ghân.


== Other versions of the legendarium ==
In all the earlier drafts of the chapter "The Ride of the Rohirrim", Ghân-buri-Ghân remained mostly the same as he is in the published text. Only his dialogue and descriptions concerning the road to Minas Tirith varied heavily in drafts. In this draft, some of his physical appearance is omitted.
In an early draft of the chapter "The Steward and the King", Ghân-buri-Ghân visits Minas Tirith with two headmen "clad in garments of green leaves" where they bow before King Elesser, who grants "them the Forest of Druadan". While writing an early draft of the chapter "Many Partings", Tolkien decided to move Aragorn's interaction with Ghân-buri-Ghân from the previous chapter. In the draft, Ghân-buri-Ghân "stood by the eaves of the trees, and did them homage as they passed". While this omits Aragorn giving them the forest, Tolkien later developed this version, re-introducing elements from the first version. In this draft, Ghân of the Wild Woods approaches Aragorn with "two of his headmen…clad all in garments of green leaves" and bow to him. After Aragorn grants them possession of the forest, they bow once more before leaving. This version was immediately replaced by a version almost resembling the published text.
