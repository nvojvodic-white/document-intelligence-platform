# Khand

Source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Khand

Khand was the name of a land which lay to the south-east of Mordor and to the east of Near Harad that was inhabited by Variags.


== History ==
Little is known about Khand or its people, other than that they were allied to Mordor. 
The people of Khand and of Rhûn seemed to have been enemies on and off throughout the ages. The Easterlings known as the Wainriders passed south of Mordor and made an alliance with the men of Khand and of Near Harad. In T.A. 1944 they made a coordinated attack against Gondor.
Years later, Variags from Khand joined the forces of Sauron during the War of the Ring.
It is unknown if Khand was ever conquered by the Reunited Kingdom or if it remained independent. It is also unknown if Khand ever warred with the folk of the West after Sauron's demise.


== Etymology ==
The meaning of Khand is unknown; it is, along with Variag, one of the few known words from the languages of the Men of the East and allies of Sauron.


== Portrayal in adaptations ==
1984-1997: Middle-earth Role Playing 

Khand is further fleshed out in the roleplaying game and its supplementary material. It is described as a hot and dry region.
The deserts and scrubland of Lower Khand were inhabited by horse herders who moved between water sources, with only a few permanent settlements along the Knife (V. "Noz Peka"), a tributary river of the Harnen. The largest of these was Sturlurtsa Khand, the traditional capital at the confluence of the Knife and the Gold-horse (V. "Medlóshad Peka").
Upper Khand, the north-eastern part of Khand, was slightly cooler. Its capital was Ûbésêsh-ûta-Pavéter (V. "Refuge from the Flailing Wind"), upstream from the Sturlurtsa Khand. Its richer clans produced many kings, whose bones were entombed in shrines in the Caves of Ôlbamarl. Ûvathar of the clan Achef, who later became a Ringwraith, was born in those caves.
