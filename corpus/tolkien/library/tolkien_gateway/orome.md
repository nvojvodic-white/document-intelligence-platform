# Oromë

Source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Orom%C3%AB

Oromë was a Vala and one of the Aratar,  also known as the Huntsman of the Valar and the Great Rider. He is considered one of the more powerful of the Valar.


== Attributes ==
Oromë was a hunter of monsters and evil creatures, riding on his steed Nahar, and blowing his great horn Valaróma. He loved horses and hounds as well as all trees and forests. In Yavanna's woods in Valinor, he trained his folk and beasts to hunt the evil creatures of Melkor. He hunted all foul beasts of Morgoth, and when he blew his horn, all would know that the woods had been purged. Oromë was known for his terrible wrath, in contrast to Tulkas, who laughed often.
Oromë was the brother of Nessa and the husband of Vána.


== History ==

During the Years of the Trees, after most of the Valar had withdrawn completely from Middle-earth and hidden themselves in Aman, Oromë was the last who came to Valinor, and even then he still hunted in the forests of Middle-earth on occasion. He visited Middle-earth during the Sleep of Yavanna, hunting monsters and fell creatures and the shadows fled temporarily until he left.
Being a powerful huntsman, he was active in the struggles against Morgoth, who feared Oromë in his anger. In efforts against Oromë, Melkor raised the Misty Mountains as an impediment to his riding, and sowed fear among the Elves of a dark Rider who, upon his wild horse, would devour Elves who wondered to far.
Oromë was the first among the Valar to find the Elves at Cuiviénen. When at length some dared to approach him, and asked his name, he answered: "Oromë". When they asked what it meant, he again answered: "Oromë. To me only is it given; for I am Oromë"; while Oromë had many glorious titles, he withheld them so that the Elves would not be afraid. When the Elves further asked the name of his horse, and if it bore any meaning, Oromë answered: "Nahar, and he is called from the sound of his voice, when he is eager to run”.
It was Oromë who gave the Elves the name "Eldar". Seeking to ensure their safety, Oromë accompanied the Elves from Cuiviénen to Beleriand. 
After the destruction of the Two Trees of Valinor, Oromë led his host in pursuit of Morgoth and Ungoliant. However, they were thwarted by Ungoliant's unlight.
Because Oromë was the only Vala who travelled in Middle-earth during the Elder Days, it was believed, even during the Third Age that the wild oxen found near the Sea of Rhun descended from his Kine.


== Legacy ==
In the aftermath of the Dagor Bragollach, Fingolfin rode to Angband alone to challenge Morgoth to single combat. Those who saw him thought Oromë himself had arrived; for a great madness of rage was upon him, so that his eyes shone like the eyes of the Valar.
During the Battle of the Pelennor Fields in the Third Age, King Théoden displayed a drive and ferocity comparable to that of Oromë.


== Etymology ==
The name Oromë is said to be derived from his Valarin name Arōmēz. 
His name is translated as "Horn-blowing" and "Sound of Horns".


=== Other names ===
Araw was the Sindarin form of the name of the Oromë, while its North Sindarin form was Arum. 
Tauron was an epithet used by the Sindar for Oromë. In the Valaquenta, Tauron is translated as "Lord of Forests". Another translation is "The Forester". The language, to which the name pertains, remains non-explicit in Tolkien's texts:

Quenya word? The root of the word could be taure ("great wood") plus the ending -on. The name would thus have the sense "One of forests".
Sindarin word? This would be suggested by its usage among the Sindar and by the Noldorin form it replaced (see below).
Béma was the name used by the Northmen for Oromë. As the great huntsman and horseman of the Valar, he and his steed Nahar were known to the horse-loving people of Rohan, who claimed that their great horses, the Mearas, had ancestors brought out of the West by Béma himself. 
The name Béma is also from the tongue of Rohan (Tolkien was inspired by the Old English word béme "trumpet").
Tolkien used at least two earlier forms of the by-name Tauron:

Tavros - the form Tauros replaced - used in such early texts as the Lay of Leithian. The name is Gnomish, defined as "Chief wood fay 'the Blue Spirit of the Woods'".
Tauros - the form Tauron replaced - is a Noldorin word defined in the Etymologies as meaning "Forest-Dread" (roots TÁWAR + GOS).
In Eriol's Old English translations, Oromë is referred to as Wáðfréa "Huntinglord", Huntena  fréa "Hunting  Lord and Lord of  Hunters" and Wealdafréa "Lord of Forests". The name Béaming is a translation of Q. Aldaron.
